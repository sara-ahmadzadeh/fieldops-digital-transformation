# Historical Project Reconstruction — Stage Conclusion

## Purpose

Understand how the company earns money from projects and determine what evidence is needed to choose a sustainable growth direction.

## What we learned

1. The company earns through both product margins and installation/repair fees. Their contribution varies by product category and project characteristics.
2. Management considers industrial and organizational customers, larger installation projects, camera projects, and outsourced execution promising growth opportunities.
3. The reconstructed historical projects provide an initial view of project economics, but the sample does not yet establish reliable profitability rankings.
4. The company's financial relationships extend beyond individual projects. Customers may retain outstanding balances while requesting new work, and the company may execute projects while owing suppliers and contractors.
5. Customer payment behavior, working-capital requirements, employee capacity, and follow-up work may materially affect which projects are attractive in practice.

## Conclusion

Historical reconstruction has provided an initial understanding of the business model and revealed the main questions that must be resolved before selecting a growth strategy.

The current evidence is not sufficient to declare one project category universally the most profitable or the best growth engine.

## Recommended next stage

Evaluate the main growth hypotheses using three dimensions:

* Project contribution and resource consumption.
* Cash requirements, payment timing, and outstanding financial obligations.
* Customer value, repeatability, and operational feasibility.

Use the results to select a viable project portfolio and translate that choice into a measurable business plan.
