# Strategic Decision Framework

## Purpose

The objective of this stage is to identify the management decisions
that could be improved through better use of operational and economic data.

The project does not begin by building dashboards or collecting data
for its own sake. It begins by identifying decisions and determining
what evidence is required to support them.

## Decision Evaluation Framework

Each candidate decision is evaluated using the following dimensions:

| Dimension | Question |
|---|---|
| Why is it important? | What business consequence does this decision have? |
| Initial hypothesis | What do we currently believe? |
| Decision type | What kind of management decision is involved? |
| Required evidence | What information would support the decision? |
| Source | Where could that information be obtained? |
| Available today? | Can the decision currently be answered with available data? |
| Priority | How valuable is it to answer this question? |

## Decision Categories

Candidate decisions will be grouped into areas such as:

- Project profitability
- Project execution
- Outsourcing
- Pricing and quotation
- Customer value
- Resource allocation
- Operational efficiency

## Principle

The transformation should prioritize decisions that:

1. have meaningful business impact;
2. occur repeatedly or have significant financial consequences;
3. can potentially be supported by reliable data;
4. lead to an actionable management response.

## Current Stage

At this stage, the decision list represents hypotheses rather than
validated conclusions. Further analysis and data availability assessment
are required before determining which decisions can be reliably supported.
