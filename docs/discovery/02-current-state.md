# Current-State Business Process

## Purpose

This document describes how the company currently operates based on the discovery conducted before historical project reconstruction.

It represents the current state ("As-Is") and should not be interpreted as a proposed future process.

---

## 1. Customer Inquiry

Customers may contact the company through:

- Office / secretary
- CEO
- Executive manager
- Occasionally technicians through previously shared personal contact information
- In-person visits to the office

The initial inquiry normally records:

- Customer contact information
- Address
- Date of inquiry
- Brief description of requested work
- Project category

For complex inquiries, the customer is referred to the CEO.

---

## 2. Site Survey

For installation projects, a site visit is part of the normal protocol.

The survey is usually performed by the executive manager and sometimes by the CEO.

The purpose is to determine:

- Required products
- Required installation work
- Site conditions
- Expected execution requirements

---

## 3. Quotation

The executive manager or CEO determines:

- Required products
- Product selling prices
- Installation requirements
- Estimated labor/time
- Service price

Product purchase prices are available to the CEO and executive manager.

The customer normally receives **one combined quotation covering products and services**.

The final quotation is approved by the CEO or executive manager.

---

## 4. Customer Approval and Deposit

After customer acceptance:

- The secretary follows up with the customer.
- The project becomes officially active.
- The secretary confirms the deposit.
- The accountant records the financial transaction.

Procurement normally begins after the deposit is received.

The executive manager determines when the project is ready for execution.

---

## 5. Procurement

Products are ordered by the CEO or executive manager.

Actual product purchases are recorded in the accounting system.

If the actual purchase price differs from the quotation:

- The customer may be informed.
- The final invoice reflects the applicable amount.

Missing or incorrect products are handled by the CEO or executive manager.

---

## 6. Scheduling and Execution

The executive manager:

- Selects technicians or installers.
- Schedules the work.
- Communicates execution requirements.

Technicians receive operational information but do not receive confidential financial information.

Technicians record execution information through standardized reports.

Since 1404, the operational Excel system and technician reports have been synchronized around standardized fields.

Typical operational information includes:

- Time
- Materials
- Work performed
- Additional work
- Problems encountered

Reports are reviewed by the executive manager or CEO.

---

## 7. Outsourced Execution

Some installation projects are outsourced because of workload or expected profitability considerations.

Two execution models have been identified.

### Model A — Installer resources

The external installer provides their own:

- Tools
- Transportation

The installer receives the agreed installation payment.

### Model B — Company resources

The external installer uses company resources such as:

- Vehicle
- Tools

The installer receives part of the installation wage.

The company retains the remainder.

The profitability of this model has not yet been established and must be investigated through historical project reconstruction.

---

## 8. Additional / Unexpected Work

Additional work may be discovered during execution.

Examples may include:

- Demolition
- Welding
- Additional wiring
- Removal of existing equipment
- Other work outside the original scope

The executive manager decides whether the additional work should be charged.

Additional work may therefore be:

- Fully billed
- Partially billed
- Absorbed by the company
- Not consistently recorded

This represents a potential source of profitability leakage.

---

## 9. Completion and Handover

The senior technician normally:

- Determines that execution is complete.
- Tests the system.
- Hands the system over to the customer.
- Completes the completion report.

The final invoice is prepared by the executive manager or CEO.

---

## 10. Payment

The accountant records financial transactions and tracks outstanding balances.

The secretary follows up with customers regarding late payments.

The CEO reviews financial information regularly and becomes involved when anomalies occur.

---

## 11. Warranty and Rework

Customer complaints normally go to the executive manager and sometimes directly to the CEO.

The executive manager and CEO determine whether the issue qualifies as warranty work.

The executive manager assigns the technician.

If the issue is caused by the company's execution, the company bears the relevant cost.

If the work is caused by a new customer request, the customer pays.

Major warranty/rework costs may be attached to the original project.

Minor warranty and callback costs are not consistently recorded.

---

# Repair Process

Repair projects differ from installations.

Typical flow:

Customer inquiry
→ Scheduling
→ Technician visit
→ Repair
→ Testing
→ Customer confirmation
→ Invoice
→ Payment follow-up

A repair may sometimes require:

- Bringing a faulty device to the company
- Sending the device to the producer for repair
- Returning the repaired device to the customer site

Most repairs are completed during the first visit.

Warranty/callbacks are relatively uncommon but follow the same principle as installation projects when they occur.

---

# Current-State Structural Problem

The company does not currently operate with a single project-level information flow.

Instead, information is distributed across several functional processes:

- Customer / secretary process
- Product pricing and procurement
- Service pricing and execution
- Technician reporting
- Accounting
- Payment follow-up
- Warranty/rework

These functions may not exist as formally separated departments, but responsibility and information access are effectively separated.

As a result, operational and financial information are not consistently connected at the project level.

This prevents reliable historical project profitability analysis from the operational data alone.

---

# Current Investigation Direction

The next stage is to reconstruct approximately 20 historical projects by connecting information across these existing processes.

The purpose is not yet to redesign the company.

The purpose is to establish what actually happened economically at project level before designing the future operating model.
