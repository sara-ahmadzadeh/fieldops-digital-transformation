# Project Origin

## 1. Initial Situation

The company had accumulated a substantial amount of operational data over several years.

The available operational data included information such as:

- customer information
- project information
- technician reports
- project dates
- installation activities
- equipment quantities
- execution information

Financial information, invoices, product purchases, and payment records were maintained separately in the company's accounting system.

The initial approach was to analyze the historical operational data and identify patterns that could help the company improve.

---

## 2. Initial Data Analysis

The existing operational data was reviewed and analyzed repeatedly.

The analysis produced observations about individual operational details, but these observations were generally minor, subjective, or difficult to translate into concrete management decisions.

The central problem was not a lack of available historical data.

The problem was that there was no clearly defined business question or management objective connecting the analysis to a specific business decision.

---

## 3. Change in Perspective

The investigation led to a fundamental question:

> Is the company primarily suffering from a lack of data analysis, or from a lack of structured business planning and connected information?

The second explanation appeared increasingly likely.

The company was collecting information, but different parts of the business were operating through separate information flows.

Operational information and financial information were not consistently connected at the project level.

---

## 4. New Direction

The project therefore shifted from:

> "Analyze the historical Excel data."

toward:

> "Understand how the business operates, determine what information is required to manage it, and reconstruct the economics of individual projects."

This changed the role of data analysis within the project.

Data analysis became a means of supporting business understanding and decision-making rather than the primary objective itself.

---

## 5. Current Objective

The current objective is to reconstruct the economic reality of individual projects by connecting:

- customer information
- project information
- quotation
- products
- purchase costs
- service revenue
- execution
- outsourcing
- additional work
- payments
- warranty and rework

The first practical step is to reconstruct approximately 20 historical projects in detail.

The results will be used to validate the business model, identify information gaps, determine the required data structure, and eventually design a better operating and information system.

---

## 6. Key Principle

The project will not assume that a particular problem is responsible for lost profitability before sufficient evidence exists.

The working principle is:

> **Hypothesis → Evidence → Finding → Decision**

Business assumptions will therefore be tested against reconstructed project data before being incorporated into the final model.
