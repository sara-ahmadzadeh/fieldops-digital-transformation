# Strategic Decision Framework

## Project phase

The project began as an operational/data-analysis initiative. During current-state discovery, the focus shifted from collecting and analyzing data to identifying the management decisions that the future information system must support.

The transformation logic is now:

**Business understanding → Strategic questions → Economic model → Data model → Future operating model → BI & execution → Impact measurement**

The purpose is not to create more reports. It is to build an evidence-based foundation for better business decisions.

---

## 1. Starting point

The company is an active security-systems integrator with established customers, technical experience, and a project-based operating model.

Its activities include:

- equipment sales
- installation and technical execution
- repair
- upgrade/development
- post-execution support

The business already has substantial operational and managerial knowledge. The initial problem was not simply a lack of data.

---

## 2. What discovery revealed

Relevant information exists across different flows:

- operational/project information
- technician reports
- quotations and sales information
- procurement/product information
- accounting, invoices, and payments
- warranty/rework information

The key discovery was that these information flows are not consistently connected at project level.

As a result, questions such as:

> How much economic value did this project actually create?

may require reconstructing information from several sources.

This means the core problem is not **data absence** but **the weak connection between information, project economics, and management decisions**.

---

## 3. The strategic shift

The project is deliberately moving away from:

> Data → analysis → dashboard

toward:

> **Decision → strategic question → evidence → economic insight → management choice → action → measurable impact**

This changes the role of data.

Data is not the destination. It is evidence required to improve decisions that matter to the future of the business.

---

## 4. Role of the historical project sample

A sample of approximately 20 detailed historical projects is being considered as a diagnostic exercise.

The sample is **not** intended to become a permanent database.

Its purpose is to test strategic hypotheses and discover:

- where economic value is created
- where economic value may be lost
- how product and service economics differ
- how execution choices affect project economics
- how additional work, rework, and warranty affect economics
- whether different customer/project types show different economic patterns
- which important questions cannot currently be answered reliably

Possible outcomes include confirmation of managerial intuition, refinement of assumptions, discovery of new patterns, identification of economic leakage, or identification of important data gaps.

---

# 5. Candidate strategic questions

These are **candidate questions for management validation**, not conclusions.

## Q1 — Growth direction

**If the objective is sustainable financial growth, which parts of the business should deliberately become the company's engines of growth?**

Decision behind the question:

> Where should the company concentrate future growth effort and resources?

Expected evidence:

- project economics
- customer/project types
- products and services
- repeat business
- execution economics

Potential strategic impact:

> More deliberate business mix and resource concentration.

---

## Q2 — Strategic customer and value proposition

**Which customers should the company seek to serve more deliberately in the future, and what value should it be known for providing to them?**

Decision behind the question:

> For whom should the company create the greatest strategic value?

Expected evidence:

- customer/project economics
- purchasing patterns
- repeat relationships
- service requirements
- payment behavior
- eventually, external market/customer evidence where needed

Potential strategic impact:

> More deliberate market focus and value proposition.

---

## Q3 — Project selection

**What characteristics make a project strategically and economically desirable, conditional, or unattractive for the company?**

Decision behind the question:

> Which projects deserve company resources?

Expected evidence:

- project economics
- complexity
- customer type
- payment conditions
- execution requirements
- risk
- capacity consumption
- outcomes of comparable projects

Potential strategic impact:

> Movement from reactive project acceptance toward deliberate project selection.

---

## Q4 — Project economic model

**How should the economics of a project work so that management can understand where value is created and where margin is lost?**

Decision behind the question:

> How should pricing, scope, execution, and direct costs be considered together?

Expected evidence:

- purchase and selling prices
- service revenue
- estimated and actual execution effort
- direct execution costs
- internal/outsourced/mixed execution
- additional work
- rework/warranty
- invoicing and collection

Potential strategic impact:

> Better pricing, estimation, and economic control.

---

## Q5 — Economic leakage

**Which recurring patterns cause the company to perform work without fully capturing the economic value created?**

Decision behind the question:

> Which sources of avoidable economic leakage should management deliberately eliminate?

Expected evidence:

- estimate vs. actual execution
- additional work
- unbilled work
- rework
- warranty
- direct costs
- pricing-related losses

Potential strategic impact:

> Protecting existing revenue and margin, not only seeking additional sales.

---

## Q6 — Scarce technical capacity

**If technical capacity is constrained, which work should receive the company's scarce internal capacity?**

Decision behind the question:

> Where should scarce technical resources be deployed, and when should capacity be supplemented externally?

Expected evidence:

- actual effort
- project economics
- complexity
- visits
- delays
- capacity constraints
- internal vs. outsourced execution

Potential strategic impact:

> Better allocation of scarce technical capacity and more deliberate use of outsourcing.

---

## Q7 — Profit-to-cash conversion

**How should project payment, deposit, and collection conditions be designed so that financial growth does not create unhealthy cash pressure?**

Decision behind the question:

> Under what financial conditions should projects be accepted and executed?

Expected evidence:

- payment terms
- deposits
- collection timing
- capital tied up before collection
- customer/project type
- economic result

Potential strategic impact:

> Better control of working capital and cash exposure.

---

# 6. Quality test for every strategic question

A candidate question should enter the transformation only if it passes these tests:

1. **Importance** — Could its answer affect the future direction or financial performance of the business?
2. **Choice** — Does it involve a real management choice between meaningful alternatives?
3. **Evidence** — Can the answer be investigated using credible evidence?
4. **Action** — Can the result become a management rule, decision, or deliberate action?
5. **Continuity** — Is the resulting knowledge valuable enough to maintain in the future information system?

### Final test

> **If we knew the answer tomorrow, what exactly would management do differently?**

If there is no meaningful answer, the question is probably analytical rather than strategic.

---

# 7. Expected output of the strategic-question phase

The output is not a list of dashboards.

It is a **Strategic Question Map** containing, for each validated question:

- the decision it supports
- current managerial knowledge and assumptions
- required evidence
- data sources
- data gaps
- analytical approach
- expected result
- management action
- business impact
- future KPI/information requirement, where justified

Only after this map is validated should the project finalize the economic model and data requirements.

---

## Portfolio principle

This repository documents the transformation logic and learning process without exposing confidential company data.

No real customer, project, financial, operational, or accounting records should be committed to the repository.
