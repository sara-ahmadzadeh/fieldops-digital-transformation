# Historical Project Economics Reconstruction

## Purpose

The company currently stores operational and financial information through separate processes.

Operational information is maintained through technician reports and the operational Excel system, 
while financial information such as purchases, invoices and payments is maintained separately through the accounting process.

Because these flows are not consistently connected at the project level, historical analysis of 
the operational data alone cannot reliably determine project profitability.

The purpose of the reconstruction is therefore to connect the available information for individual historical projects.

---

## Reconstruction Unit

The primary reconstruction unit is the **project**.

For each selected project, information is reconstructed across the following lifecycle:

Customer inquiry
→ Site survey
→ Quotation
→ Customer approval and deposit
→ Procurement
→ Scheduling
→ Execution
→ Additional work
→ Completion
→ Invoice
→ Payment
→ Warranty / rework

Installation and repair projects are both included, but their operational patterns are kept distinguishable.

---

## Economic Components

Each project is examined through three economic views:

### 1. Product Economics

- Product selling revenue
- Actual purchase cost
- Product quantities
- Product margin

### 2. Service Economics

- Service revenue
- Internal execution costs
- Outsourced installer payments
- Transportation
- Company resources
- Other direct execution costs

### 3. Total Project Economics

The complete project view combines product and service economics and accounts for additional work, warranty/rework and other directly attributable costs.

The purpose is to determine whether a project was economically successful as a whole, rather than evaluating product and service activity in isolation.

---

## Execution Models

Historical projects must distinguish between:

### Internal execution

Company technicians perform the work using company resources.

### Outsourced execution — installer resources

An external installer performs the installation using their own tools and transportation and receives the agreed installation payment.

### Outsourced execution — company resources

An external installer performs the installation using company resources such as vehicles and tools.

The installer receives an agreed portion of the installation revenue and the company retains the remainder.

The profitability of these models is treated as a hypothesis to be tested rather than an assumption.

---

## Additional Work

Extra work is recorded separately because it may:

- have been outside the original quotation;
- create additional cost;
- be billed to the customer;
- be partially billed;
- be absorbed by the company.

Multiple extra-work records may therefore belong to a single project.

The reconstruction aims to measure the gap between work performed, work billed and cost absorbed.

---

## Warranty and Rework

Warranty and rework are reconstructed as separate events because their costs may occur after the original execution.

The analysis should distinguish, where evidence exists, between:

- execution error;
- product failure;
- customer-requested additional work;
- other causes;
- unknown cause.

Minor warranty or callback work may historically have gone unrecorded. Such gaps are themselves recorded as information-quality findings rather than silently estimated.

---

## Customer Dimensions

The reconstruction captures:

- Customer type
- Customer relationship

Initial customer types include:

- Residential
- Commercial
- Organizational
- Industrial
- Other

Customer segmentation is currently an analytical dimension, not a proven business segmentation strategy.

Its usefulness will be evaluated after sufficient projects have been reconstructed.

---

## Information Source Mapping

For each important data element, the reconstruction records:

**Information → Where it exists → Exact record → Who records it → Who can access it → Can it be connected to the project?**

This is necessary because the project is not only investigating economics.

It is also investigating the company's information architecture.

---

## Data Quality Principle

The reconstruction must distinguish between:

- Known information
- Unknown information
- Estimated information
- Conflicting information

Unknown values should not be silently replaced with assumptions.

The absence of information is itself a finding because it may identify a weakness in the current business process.

---

## Expected Outcome

The reconstruction of approximately 20 historical projects should allow the project to determine:

1. Whether project-level economic visibility is currently possible.
2. Which costs and revenues can actually be connected to projects.
3. Where information is lost between departments or processes.
4. Whether product and service economics behave differently.
5. Whether execution models differ economically.
6. The significance of additional unbilled work.
7. The significance of warranty and rework.
8. Whether repair services appear economically sustainable.
9. Which information must be captured in the future.
10. What the future business and data model should contain.

The final model will be based on evidence from reconstructed projects rather than assumptions made before the investigation.
