# Business Model Hypothesis

## Status

**Status:** Preliminary — requires validation through historical project reconstruction.

This document describes the current understanding of the company's business model based on discovery interviews and process investigation.

It is intentionally treated as a hypothesis rather than a final business model.

---

# 1. Business Model Overview

The company operates as a security systems integrator and field-service business.

Its activities combine:

1. Product sales
2. Installation services
3. Repair services
4. Warranty/rework
5. Project management and customer support

The company therefore does not operate purely as a product retailer or purely as a service provider.

Its economic model is a combination of **product revenue and service revenue**.

# 11. Core Business Logic

The current business can be simplified as:

```text
Customer Demand
      ↓
Survey / Diagnosis
      ↓
Quotation
      ↓
Customer Approval + Deposit
      ↓
Product Procurement
      ↓
Service Execution
      ↓
Completion
      ↓
Invoice
      ↓
Payment
      ↓
Warranty / Rework if required
```
Economically, the project contains at least two interconnected flows:
- PRODUCT FLOW
    Purchase → Product → Customer → Product Revenue

- SERVICE FLOW
    Labor / Execution → Customer → Service Revenue

The two flows are combined from the customer's perspective because the customer normally receives one combined quotation.

The central analytical question is therefore whether they should also be evaluated as one integrated project economically.

# 12. Outsourcing Economics

Outsourcing is not automatically considered profitable or unprofitable.

The company currently uses outsourcing as a capacity and project-selection mechanism.

The economic outcome depends on the execution model.

For outsourced projects using company resources, the analysis must determine whether:
Service Revenue
− Installer Payment
− Vehicle Cost
− Tool / Resource Cost
− Other Direct Costs

produces an acceptable contribution.
This is currently an unanswered business question.

# 13. Important Business Hypotheses

The following hypotheses require evidence.

H1 — Product and service economics should be evaluated separately.

This allows management to understand the contribution of each component.

H2 — Product and service economics should also be evaluated together.

The customer purchases a combined solution, and therefore the project-level result may differ from the result of either component alone.

H3 — Additional unbilled work may reduce project profitability.

This must be measured through historical projects.

H4 — Warranty and rework may create hidden costs.

Minor visits may currently escape economic measurement.

H5 — Outsourced execution may produce different economics from internal execution.

The two outsourcing models should be compared.

H6 — Repair services may not all have the same economic result.

Repair profitability should be analyzed by relevant characteristics rather than assumed to be break-even.

H7 — Customer type and relationship may explain differences in project economics.

This is a hypothesis, not an established fact.

# 14. Critical Business Question

The central question of the transformation is not:

"How can the company analyze more data?"

It is:

"How can the company understand the economic result of the work it performs and use that understanding to make better operational and commercial decisions?"

Historical project reconstruction is the first major step toward answering this question.
